watchduck // every tick computed by DuckDB, in your browser — 3D

A Swiss lever escapement built the “model the metal, not the maths” way: parts are polygons, DuckDB-WASM asks do these shapes overlap? on a 97 × 49 wheel-phase × fork-angle grid (4,753 states — one verdict per cell, each from 2 ST_Intersects) to build the collision map, then one prepared SQL query per frame walks the map tick by tick. No physics engine — just SQL. The 3D is pure presentation — DuckDB still decides every tick — nothing in the code says “12° per beat”; that number falls out of the shapes. Drag to orbit, scroll to zoom — flip it over to see the dial and hands on the other side.

Break the Watch · one real parameter ▾
The entry pallet stone, shifted along its long axis (mm). Applying a value regenerates the stone's WKT, rebuilds esc_map (97 × 49 wheel-phase × fork-angle grid, 2 ST_Intersects per cell) and re-prepares p_frame in DuckDB — and the 3D stone, wireframe overlay and take-apart profile are rendered from that same parameter. 0.00 = the frozen baseline (scripts/geometry.py).

two different clocks: the query frequency is fixed by the 8 ms virtual physics substep (125 queries/s while the sim runs at 1×), while the frame rate is measured from requestAnimationFrame intervals in this tab. On a slow machine one query can span several frames — the two numbers are not interchangeable.

What SQL computes vs what Three.js draws

SQL (DuckDB-WASM + Spatial) computes: the collision map (ST_Intersects over the 97 × 49 wheel × fork grid), the per-frame blocked / entry / exit verdicts (p_frame), the 3D-solid clash cross-check, and the take-apart order. Three.js only draws: those same polygons as metal meshes, their rotations, and the highlight state taken from the SQL verdicts — no physics, no collision math in the renderer.
